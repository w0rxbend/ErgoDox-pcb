ErgoDox RP2040 Either half leads Design Tech specs Progress View on GitHub

ErgoDox, rewired.

The split keyboard you know, rebuilt around an RP2040 in each half. ZMK firmware. USB-C on every port. A light under every key.

In development. The boards have not been fabricated yet.

Either half can lead.

Both boards carry the same controller, the same ports and the same circuit. Plug the computer into the side you prefer. That half becomes the master and powers the other one over the link cable.

Computer plugged into
Left
Central. Talks to the computer and powers the link.
Right
Peripheral. Sends its keys over the link.

ZMK fixes the role when the firmware is built, so each half is flashed with a central or a peripheral image. Swap sides by reflashing both halves over USB. Nothing is soldered and no jumper moves.

Two cables. Both USB-C.

One ordinary USB-C cable goes to the computer. A second one joins the halves. No TRRS jack, no mini-USB, no adapter board.

A link that cannot be mistaken for a USB port

The link carries a serial connection between the two controllers, not USB. It only supplies power after it detects the other half, and the board is marked so nobody plugs a computer into it.

A controller in each half

Each board has its own RP2040, 16 MB of flash and a 12 MHz crystal. The I/O expander and the Teensy are gone. A button on each half starts the USB bootloader.

Light under every key

Each switch has its own RGB LED, mounted on the back and shining up through the board. Brightness is capped in firmware so both halves stay inside what one USB port may supply.

Switches you can change

Every key sits in a hotswap socket. Pull a switch out and push another one in. The fab assembles the sockets, diodes and LEDs, so there is no SMD soldering at home.

The case you already own

Switch centers, mounting holes and stabilizers stay on the original ErgoDox coordinates. All 38 switch positions per half match the Litster acrylic plate exactly.

Open from schematic to firmware

The KiCad projects are generated by scripts in the repository, so every placement rule is written down and repeatable. The ZMK shield lives beside them.

Tech specs

Keys
76 in total, 38 per half. MX hotswap sockets. Two 2u thumb keys per half with PCB-mount stabilizers.
Controller
Raspberry Pi RP2040 in each half, with 16 MB QSPI flash and a 12 MHz crystal.
Firmware
ZMK, wired split over UART. Four images: left or right, central or peripheral.
Computer port
USB-C, USB 2.0 full speed, with ESD protection.
Link port
USB-C receptacle carrying UART at 1 Mbaud and 5 V. Uses a standard USB 2.0 C-to-C cable.
Matrix
6 rows by 7 columns per half, one 1N4148W diode per key, wired straight to the RP2040.
Lighting
One SK6812MINI-E per key, reverse mounted, on a switched 5 V rail. Capped at 10% of full white.
Power
Bus powered, planned for 500 mA from one port for both halves. 3.3 V from an AP2112K regulator.
Board
4 layers, 1.6 mm. Sockets, diodes, LEDs and the controller on the back. Connectors and buttons on the front.
Case
Fits the Litster ErgoDox acrylic case, Standard plate. The USB-C openings have not been test fitted.
Design tools
KiCad 10 projects generated from Python. Parts chosen from the JLCPCB assembly library.

Where the project is.

This is a design in progress, not a product you can order. Nothing has been manufactured or tested on real hardware.

  1. Done

    Schematics

    Both halves pass the electrical rule check with no errors.

  2. Done

    Placement

    All parts placed on both boards with no design rule errors. Switch positions match the case plates.

  3. In progress

    Routing

    Copper is being routed and checked. The boards are not ready to fabricate until this passes with zero errors.

  4. To do

    Fabrication files

    Gerbers, bill of materials and placement files for assembly.

  5. To do

    First boards

    Build a pair, flash ZMK, and test every key, both ports and the lighting.
